# Changelog

What changed in each release. Releases are built by `.github/workflows/release.yml` when a commit message
contains `[release]` (see Run and build in `docs/reference.md`). Newest first.

## Unreleased

- **Chimera Downs: colliders for what you can reach, and every building edge grinds.** With trick thrust the roofs are in reach, and they had no colliders: you flew through the stands' roofs, the arcades, the gate halls, the clubhouse, the drum, the stables. `tools/downs-audit.mjs coverage` (new) samples every solid surface and tests it against the live colliders: 145,000 of 412,000 m2 had none within 0.35 m; now 15,400 (the seats, on purpose; the arcades' ceilings, 0.4 m under their collided deck; arch tops, lamp heads, festoons and the wheel's lattice).
  - **Colliders from the Blender parts' own geometry**: a part built with `parts(..., solid=True)` (`tools/blender/downs_common.py`) writes a `col` empty for each primitive big enough to hit (12 cm thick, 1 m long): boxes exactly and turned with them (a raked roof deck is a raked box), upright cylinders and lathes as cylinders, an extruded outline as its bounding box when it fills 60% of it, anything else as the box round it; glow, glass, beams, tubes and lettering never collide, nor do floors and plinths (under 0.9 m high and 2.5 m across: the runner steps up only about 0.4 m, so a slab he used to walk across would stop him dead). `downsKit.bake` places them wherever the part goes (`Kit.solidQ`, new, for turned boxes), so they follow the model when it changes. 30 parts across eight Downs files are solid (roofs, towers, suites, entries, the clubhouse, arcade bays, gate halls, the main gate, the fountain, benches, planters, the finish arch, photo tower and masts, the wheel frame, the carousel base, the ticket hall, kiosks, the fair gate, the drum and podiums, the stables, the hay barn, the pavilion, hay, picnic tables, troughs, the yard gates, the stalls); the back walls (whose openings a bounding box would close), the seats, the crowd and the rides keep hand-placed colliders or none. The arcades' roofs are code-made: `arcadeShell` gives each bay's deck a raked box. `tools/downs-audit.mjs blocked` (new) lists walk-height ground the new colliders take: counters, benches, bales, gate posts and column footings, nothing in a way through.
  - **Long colliders bend**: on the curved Downs `world.col` cuts a box longer than 8 m into pieces, each bent at its own middle as the Kit bends geometry; one straight 230 m box sagged nearly 2 m off the stand roof at its ends. Landing tests: the main stand's roof holds you at 24.6 m in the middle and at its end, the far stand's at 15.6 m, an arcade's at 8.2 m. The Downs has 6,815 colliders (was 1,213); a player step there costs about 0.23 ms (0.07 ms in the open).
  - **LEDGE GRIND**: at the Downs the top edges of the colliders are grind rails (`src/ledges.js`, new): every level-topped box's four edges, sampled each metre, kept where nothing sits on them, nothing stands against them (not a wall's foot) and no neighbour carries the surface on (not a seam), at least 0.3 m up; edges meeting end to end in line are joined, 2.5 m or longer kept: 900 rails, 17.7 km, from kerbs to the 26 m roof ridge, the clubhouse canopy among them. They're worked out as you come within 400 m, a few milliseconds a frame (`Grinder.ledgeStep`), into their own lookup grid. A ledge only takes you **on skates, from the air, heading along it** (within about 40 degrees, 1.5 m reach): no tip scoop and no hop-on from the side, so running along a roof, landing across an edge or stepping off a counter never snags. Grinding one shows LEDGE GRIND on the style meter (a grind, like the rocks and rims).
  - The Asset Row builds the changed Downs parts (228 items, none failing); the fountain's Row model skips its colliders.

- **Style combos and the style meter.** Every style move now feeds one **run** (`src/combo.js`, new): flips and spins, air (BIG AIR from 5 s), dive landings, grinds (GRIND on snake rocks, RIM GRIND on crater lips), meteor bounces, and two new moves: **ARCH THREAD** (35, skating through a rock arch's opening either way: `archPass` in `src/rocks.js`; the arches are kept per chunk in `planet.arches`) and **HITCH-LINE** (7 a second while hitched at 50 m/s or more, a little faster than a trick's 5 a second of air; committed when you let go). The run stays open in the air, on a rail or hitched, and for 2.5 s after you land, so a jump off the end of a grind or straight after a landing carries it on; then it **banks**: the sum of its moves times the **multiplier** (+0.5 for each kind of move past the first; air time doesn't count as a kind) goes into the style pool, and a delivery banks an open run before it pays. A wipeout, or any crash into a ragdoll, loses the run. A clean trick landed onto a rail now counts (it was dropped). Best trick in the stats is now the best run.
  - **The style meter** (`src/stylemeter.js`, new; bottom right, mirroring the speedometer, after Ultrakill's) shows the run as it builds: a rank letter that climbs with the total (D USTY, C OSMIC from 150, B LASTOFF 350, A POLLO 700, S TELLAR 1200, SS TARBURST 2000, SSS UPERMOON 3200) and a bar to the next rank, the newest six moves (repeats merge: METEOR BOUNCE ×3) with their points, the multiplier, the total, CLEAN / CROOKED while a trick is in the air, and a bar draining through the landing window. It ends on BANKED! (green) or WIPEOUT (red, struck through), held 3.2 s before it fades.
  - **No more trick popups**: STUCK IT!, WIPEOUT!, the trick names, BIG AIR, DIVE LANDING, GRIND! and METEOR BOUNCE / CHAIN all show on the meter instead. Every other popup stays (speed marks, BOING!, LAUNCH!, SKY-HIGH!, ALL ABOARD!, KNOCKED OFF!, rams, kills, story events).
  - Trick scoring is one function, `trickScore` in `src/player.js`, for the live view and the landing. A single flip is called BACKFLIP (it was 1x BACKFLIP).

- **Trick thrust: the jetpack pushes the way your body faces mid-trick.** Turned in a trick (hold `Q`), the thrusters push along the body's own forward axis instead of where you aim: a quarter backflip (lying face up) pushes you up, a quarter frontflip drives you down, a half spin is an air brake. The push follows the pose, blending in from upright to all the pack's way by 60° off, at 70% of the thrusters' power (`TRICK_THRUST` in `src/player.js`). The jet tank is the only limit: measured in the game at 60 Hz, one second of thrust in a quarter backflip leaves you rising 4.5 m/s where you would have fallen 6, and a full tank (3.3 s) adds about 35 m/s of upward speed. The jet sparks follow the push.

- **Seeker: a gentler, smarter pulse, and the mortar and scattergun seek too.** The pulse disc's turn rate was 0.9 + 1.3 × level rad/s (0.9 to 4.8): snappy at the top, weak at the bottom. It is now 1.5 + 0.7 × level (1.5, 2.2, 2.9, 3.6: a 77, 52, 40 and 32 m turn radius at 115 m/s). A volley locks **one target as it leaves** (`pickLock` in `src/projectiles.js`: inside the aim's cone, within range, **in line of sight**, the nearest to the aim first) and keeps it, instead of every disc rescanning every enemy every frame; a shot that loses sight of its target coasts. It steers by **proportional navigation** (3 × the line of sight's turn rate, read from the target's motion, capped at the Seeker's rate), so it curves to where a crossing target will be and not to where it is: in a test, a disc at the base rate hit a target crossing at 15 m/s (0.6 m miss) where an unguided one missed by 65 m. **Mortar** shells take 0.35 × the rate, steering about the vertical only so they keep their arc, with a wide cone and 300 m; **scattergun** pellets 0.12 × (about 10° to 25° a second over a 1.15 s pellet life), one lock per volley. The Seeker's descriptions say so.

- **Your shots keep up with you.** A player's shot left at its base speed plus half or 0.6 of your velocity in any direction, so at speed it drifted off the crosshair and, going fast enough, you outran it. Now it flies dead along your aim at the base speed plus the part of your velocity along that aim (`launch` in `src/weapons.js`): moving toward your target the shot always leaves at the base speed relative to you, moving away or sideways changes nothing. The pulse spinner, scattergun, mortar and APC cannon all use it. Shots at 400+ m/s would have skipped thin walls (a projectile was tested for ground and colliders only where it stood each frame), so the frame's flight is now swept in steps of at most 1 m, up to 40, and a shot stops where it first touches (`update` in `src/projectiles.js`); and a shot has a `range` (its base speed times its old life: 460 m pulse, 200 m scatter, 600 m mortar, 544 m cannon) after which it bursts, so a fast shot reaches no farther than a slow one did.
